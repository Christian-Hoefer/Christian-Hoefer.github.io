Stages of Development

Game development goes through a series of stages, from the conception of an idea to years of updates after release. Click a stage below to jump to it.

  1. Planning
  2. Pre-production
  3. Production
  4. Testing
  5. Pre-launch
  6. Launch
  7. Post-production

1. Planning

Planning has two main phases usually, Game ideation and Proofing a concept. Game ideation is one of the most important parts of development in that it sets expectations and will create the foundation on which the game is further developed. This will involve planning the type or genre of game, the mechanics, the characters, the background of the game, the platform for development, or key features of the game. This can be simple concepts that will drive the creation of the game forward.

Proofing the concept takes all of the ideas produced from the Game Ideation step and checks viability of the ideas that were produced. It will answer questions like the cost of development, the engine needed for development, how many people will be needed to produce this game along with more practicality questions on producing the video game.

2. Pre-production

This stage is for creating the scope of the project with their resources. Writers will create the main characters, their backstories, relations to other characters. Engineers will give technical restraints to the plans and what scenes are capabilities they will have for following the plans created by the writers and designers. Artists will collaborate with designers to cement art styles, color palettes are present throughout the game and follow initial planning or change if needed. Developers will meet with engineers to flesh out game mechanics, and how the game will portray different elements in the engine. Project Leads will meet with multiple different departments to find the "fun" in the game and it could still take time up until testing to find this.

3. Production

This is the longest stage of development and where most of the effort is placed into creating a video game. This process could be years at a time depending on the size and scope of the studio. Character models are created, Audio Engineers design every sound that you will hear in the game including the steps you make or how you interact with the environment. Level designers craft the environments and surfaces that you will interact with as you move through the game. Voice actors will record lines for the characters and the emotions from the script of the game. Developers will write the code to bring every piece of content come to life and run the game on various platforms.

4. Testing

Testing is an important part of the game development because entire portions of the game that were developed can be removed or scrapped entirely for a number of reasons. Every part of the game needs to be tested if the studio wants the game to have quality assurance and also test the game for that "fun" factor.

5. Pre-launch

This stage can be when a Beta is created for public testing and marketing starts to ramp up or start if the studio has less funds at this stage. Gameplay trailers are released during this phase or early copies are sent to people for them to showcase the game instead.

6. Launch

The biggest moment of the game's development where the game is officially launched and released to the public. Before launch months of time are spent on bug-fixing and polishing the game to a higher standard. Modern game development spends less time on this stage and puts more into post-production.

7. Post-production

Post-launch is the time after where the game is supported beyond its launch. In traditional single-player or offline games this period can be a shorter time of polishing the game or extend by further content that is added to the game in a DLC. Multiplayer releases can be in post production for many years as content is added continuously and on a regular cadence creating a perpetual development cycle.
